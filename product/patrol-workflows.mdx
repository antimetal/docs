A [Patrol](/product/patrols) is only useful if someone acts on what it finds. This page shows when to run which Patrol, and what to do with the results.

## The loop

Every workflow below is the same four steps:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Scope it">
    Pick a template and point it at the services and environments you care about. Narrow beats broad.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run it">
    Select **Run now**, or set a schedule. Creating a Patrol does not start a run.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Act on the findings">
    Fix the Issues first: something is wrong now. Then pick the Improvements worth doing. Open a pull request from the finding, or hand it to your coding agent.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Steer it">
    Comment on findings that missed the mark, and use **Steer** to say what to focus on or ignore. Later runs follow both.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Choose a workflow

| Workflow | Template | Schedule | Who acts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Before a launch](#before-a-launch-or-traffic-event) | Service health check, Database health check | Run now, then every few hours during the event | The launch owner |
| [After an incident](#after-an-incident) | Service health check | Daily for two weeks | The incident owner |
| [Weekly review](#weekly-review) | Service and Database health checks | Weekly, before planning | The team, in planning |
| [Catch what monitors miss](#catch-what-your-monitors-miss) | Error sweep | Every 4 hours | On-call |
| [After a deploy](#after-a-deploy) | The Patrol that covers what changed | Run now, from your agent | The engineer who shipped |

## Before a launch or traffic event

**Use when:** you ship a big feature, start a marketing push, or expect a traffic spike.

1. A week before, create a **Service health check** for each service on the launch path, in production. Add the **API Endpoints** the launch depends on. Leave the schedule off.
2. Create a **Database health check** for each Postgres database those services use.
3. Select **Run now** on each. Work through the Issues first, then the Improvements you can land before launch day.
4. The day before, select **Run now** again to confirm the fixes held.
5. For launch week, open **Configure** and turn on a recurring schedule, such as every 3 hours during business hours (`0 9-17/3 * * 1-5`). Archive the Patrols when the launch is over, and restore them for the next one.

Name the event in **Additional instructions**, so findings are judged against it:

```text
We launch the new checkout on Friday and expect 5x normal traffic on POST /checkout.
Flag anything that would not hold at that load.
```

## After an incident

**Use when:** an [Issue](/product/issue-lifecycle) is resolved and you want to make sure it doesn't come back.

1. Create a **Service health check** on the service that failed, running daily.
2. Describe the incident in **Additional instructions**:

   ```text
   On Nov 3, connection pool exhaustion took down orders-api for 40 minutes.
   Watch pool saturation and retry storms on orders-api and its callers.
   ```

3. Read each run. Runs compare with the previous run, so the report says whether a problem continues, gets worse or clears up.
4. After two clean weeks, change the schedule to weekly, or archive the Patrol.

## Weekly review

**Use when:** you want a standing check on the systems your team owns, without waiting for alerts.

1. Create a **Service health check** for each critical service and a **Database health check** for each production Postgres database. Schedule them weekly, the morning before your planning meeting.
2. Turn on the [Patrol digest](/product/patrols#get-patrol-results-in-slack) and post it to your team's channel just after the runs finish. It summarizes the top findings and fixes.
3. In planning, go through the digest. Open each finding that matters, assign the fix, or open the pull request.
4. Comment on findings that weren't useful, so next week's runs are sharper.

## Catch what your monitors miss

**Use when:** you're not sure your alerts cover what matters.

Create an **Error sweep** on your production services, with a cron of `0 */4 * * *`. Each run looks back 4 hours, so this covers every hour once. It finds errors no monitor catches, surfaces anomalies, and recommends monitors you should have.

Treat its Improvements as your monitoring backlog: each one names the monitor to add and why.

## After a deploy

**Use when:** you just shipped and want to know it's healthy before you move on.

From your coding agent, after the deploy finishes:

```text
Use Antimetal to run the service health check for checkout-svc,
wait for it to finish, and fix anything it flags in this repo.
```

## From your coding agent

Every workflow above works from Claude Code, Cursor or any client connected to the [Antimetal MCP server](/agents/mcp). Ask in plain language:

| You ask | Your agent calls |
| --- | --- |
| "Set up a daily database health check on orders-db in production." | `create_patrol` |
| "Run the checkout service health check now and tell me what it finds." | `run_patrol`, then `get_patrol_run` once the run completes |
| "What did my patrols find this week that nobody picked up?" | `list_events` for Patrol findings that are not linked to anything |
| "Fix the top finding from last night's run and open a PR." | `get_patrol_run`, then `get_issue_fixes` or `get_improvement_fixes` |
| "Tell the error sweep to ignore 404s from the legacy API." | `update_patrol` |
| "Finding F3 was noise: those errors are expected during deploys." | `submit_patrol_feedback` |
| "Run the health check every weekday at 8am Pacific." | `update_patrol_schedule` |
| "Archive the launch-week patrols." | `update_patrol_status` |

## Tips

- **One Patrol, one question.** Several narrow Patrols beat one broad one. They are easier to read and to steer.
- **Name the stakes.** "This service handles payments" in the instructions changes what counts as serious.
- **Match the cadence to the window.** The Error sweep looks back 4 hours; the health checks look back 7 days.
- **Comment, don't just vote.** Antimetal learns from what you write on a finding, not from the thumbs alone.
- **Archive, don't delete.** Archiving stops runs and keeps the history, so you can restore the Patrol next time.
